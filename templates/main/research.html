Turning scattered reports and ransom notes into a structured way to recognize coercion.

I have been part of the APRIORI Center since this project began. The lab is led by my mentor, Dr. Bhupendra Acharya, with whom I continue to work. I began the project as an individual researcher under his mentorship. I developed the data collection, cleaning, analysis, and visualization work behind the study.

The codebook framework organizes recurring manipulation tactics, language patterns, and behavioral indicators. Its purpose is to support awareness and further research into how blackmail threats are communicated.

Questions that guided the work

  • What motivations and pressures may shape blackmail scamming?
  • Which communication strategies, threats, and persuasive tactics recur?
  • Which vulnerabilities do these messages attempt to exploit?

02 / Methods

From source material to patterns.

Collect

Gather public reports from ChainAbuse and Reddit alongside ransom notes from Ransomware.live.

Clean

Filter duplicate, incomplete, and irrelevant entries; remove HTML markup and boilerplate text.

Analyze

Examine threat language, emotional framing, payment demands, and urgency using thematic analysis and keyword tracking.

Organize

Bring recurring tactics and linguistic indicators into a codebook framework for comparison.

03 / Observations from the original study

Different messages.
Recurring pressure.

The reviewed material showed recurring exposure threats, payment urgency, and claims of control. Victim accounts emphasized fear and uncertainty, while ransomware notes often used a more formal structure with payment instructions and escalation warnings.

These are observations from the studied sources. Public reports are selective, and messages alone cannot establish a scammer's internal motivations or a definitive victim profile.

04 / Ongoing team research

Exploring the connections.

I am continuing this work with a team at the APRIORI Center during my graduate studies, under the continued mentorship of Dr. Bhupendra Acharya. We are investigating new angles and connections to blackmail scamming, building on the original research.

A working map of research directions

The current visual connects blackmail scamming with sextortion, romance scams, ransomware, impersonation scams, and investment fraud often called pig-butchering.

We are using these connections to frame further investigation. The map represents work in progress, rather than a set of established findings or a claim that these categories are interchangeable.
